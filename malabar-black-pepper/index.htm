What is Malabar black pepper?

Malabar black pepper comes from the Malabar coast of South India, in and around Kerala, one of the world's best-known pepper-growing regions. Pepper from this coast has been traded for centuries and is valued for its bold aroma, warm heat and clean finish in both everyday cooking and professional kitchens.

How to use black pepper

Use whole peppercorns in rasam, biryani, pulao, curries, pickles, marinades, stocks and masala chai. Crush or grind them fresh just before cooking for the strongest aroma, or toast them lightly in a dry pan to deepen the flavour. Ground pepper suits table use, rubs and quick seasoning.

Whole vs ground black pepper

Whole peppercorns keep their aroma and heat for much longer, because the flavour compounds stay sealed inside until you crush them. Ground pepper is convenient but fades faster. For everyday cooking, keep whole peppercorns and grind small amounts as needed.

How to store black pepper

Store pepper in an airtight container, away from heat, moisture and direct sunlight. Always use a dry spoon. Stored this way, whole peppercorns stay fragrant far longer than ground pepper.

What is Malabar black pepper?

Malabar black pepper is pepper (Piper nigrum) grown along the Malabar coast of South India, in and around Kerala. It has been traded for centuries and is known for its bold aroma and warm, lingering heat.

Why is black pepper sun-dried?

After harvest, the green pepper berries are dried in the sun until they darken and wrinkle. Sun-drying is the traditional method and is what turns the berries into familiar black peppercorns.

Should I buy whole or ground black pepper?

Whole peppercorns keep their aroma longer and can be freshly crushed or ground just before cooking. Ground pepper is more convenient but loses aroma faster.

How should I store black pepper?

Keep it in an airtight container away from heat, moisture and direct sunlight. Whole peppercorns stay fresh much longer than ground pepper.
