Malabar vs Tellicherry Pepper: What Is the Difference?

Shoppers often see both "Malabar" and "Tellicherry" on pepper labels and wonder whether they are the same thing. The short answer is that the names describe different things, and they are closely connected.

What "Malabar" means

Malabar is the name of the coastal region of South India, in and around Kerala, where pepper has been grown and traded for centuries. "Malabar black pepper" refers to pepper from this region.

What "Tellicherry" means

Tellicherry (today usually written Thalassery) is a town on the Malabar coast. The name has come to describe a premium, larger-berry grade of pepper traditionally associated with that area. Larger, well-developed berries are generally prized for their aroma.

How they relate

Tellicherry is best understood as a grade within Malabar pepper, not a completely separate pepper. Grades are usually defined by berry size, density and cleanliness. Standard grades are often sold as "garbled" pepper, which means it has been cleaned and sieved to remove light berries and foreign matter.

How to choose

  • Everyday cooking, rubs and masalas: a good-quality Malabar black pepper works well.
  • Table grinders and finishing: larger, bold berries are often preferred for their aroma.
  • Business buyers: ask the supplier for the grade, size and packing details before ordering.
